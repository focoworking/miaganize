It's 8 p.m. on a Tuesday in Brickell and the rooftop is full. Nobody is talking about the sunset. A founder is pitching an AI underwriting tool to two investors in linen, a hotel executive is asking how to get her property to show up when travelers ask a chatbot where to stay, and somewhere a student from Miami Dade College is handing out a QR code. This is Miami tech in 2026: less "move here and tweet about it," more "build here and sell to the world."

**The short answer:** Miami's tech and AI scene in 2026 is bigger, more serious and more AI-heavy than it was during the 2021 hype wave. South Florida startups raised **$4.13 billion in venture capital in 2025**, up 49% from 2024, according to the [eMerge Insights report covered by Refresh Miami](https://refreshmiami.com/news/miami-area-startups-snag-4-13b-in-vc-in-2025-new-emerge-insights-report-has-the-details/). Palantir moved its headquarters here in February 2026, local colleges are minting AI graduates, and the region's flagship conference, eMerge Americas, returns **March 2–4, 2027**.

## What is eMerge Americas, and when is the next one?

[eMerge Americas](https://www.emergeamericas.com/) is the anchor of Miami's tech calendar, a conference and expo founded by Manny and Melissa Medina that bridges the U.S., Latin America and Europe. The 2026 edition, the event's 12th, ran April 22–24 at the Miami Beach Convention Center. Ahead of it, the [City of Miami Beach](https://www.miamibeachfl.gov/miami-beach-leads-the-tech-conversation-emerge-america-returns/) said it expected more than 20,000 attendees from over 60 countries. National security, AI and quantum workshops filled the days before the main stage opened.

The big news for planners: the official site lists **eMerge Americas 2027 for March 2–4, 2027**, again at the Miami Beach Convention Center. That's about seven weeks earlier than the 2026 dates.

!! March 2–4, 2027: eMerge Americas moves up from its usual April slot

### And Miami Tech Week?

"Miami Tech Week" is less a single event than a swarm: a week of community-organized side events, happy hours, demo days and fund dinners that orbit eMerge. In 2026 that orbit also included Israel Tech Week Miami (April 27–30) and other gatherings scattered across downtown. As of this writing we found no formal announcement of the 2027 edition, so if you're planning travel, anchor on the eMerge dates and watch community calendars like Refresh Miami's for the side events.

## How much money is flowing into Miami startups?

More than at any point outside the 2021 peak, and a growing share of it is AI. According to the eMerge Insights figures for 2025:

| Metric (2025) | Figure |
|---|---|
| South Florida VC raised | $4.13 billion, 376 deals |
| Change vs. 2024 | +49% |
| Florida statewide | about $5.8 billion, 575 deals |
| AI-focused startups, Miami metro | $1.23 billion (vs. $897 million in 2024) |
| Fintech, South Florida | $909 million, 85 deals |
| Healthcare, South Florida | $821 million, 56 deals |

!! $1.23 billion: VC raised in 2025 by AI-focused startups in the Miami metro

The single largest South Florida deal of 2025 was a $200 million round for OpenEvidence, the clinical-AI platform used by doctors, which relocated from Cambridge, Massachusetts, to Miami that year, according to the same report. Quarterly numbers from PitchBook get revised often, so treat any single quarter as a snapshot, not a verdict.

## Which big companies have moved here?

The headline move of 2026 was **Palantir**, which said in February that it had moved its headquarters from Denver to Miami, as [The Real Deal reported](https://therealdeal.com/miami/2026/02/17/ai-firm-palantir-real-estate-firm-trinity-move-hqs-to-miami). It's the data-analytics company's second relocation; it left Palo Alto for Denver in 2020.

Smaller but telling: AI-first field-service software company Simpro set its North American headquarters at One Downtown, and AI compliance startup Comp AI opened its headquarters in Aventura. The pattern isn't trophy-office tourism anymore. These are operating teams, sales hubs and engineering pods.

## Can you actually study AI in Miami?

Yes, and that matters more than any relocation headline, because talent is what keeps companies here.

- **Miami Dade College** received state approval on July 19, 2023, to offer [Florida's first Bachelor of Science in Applied Artificial Intelligence](https://news.mdc.edu/?p=14518), which launched in 2024. It sits on top of an associate degree and college credit certificates in AI, and doesn't require a programming background to start.
- **FIU** offers a B.S. in Data Science and AI through its Knight Foundation School of Computing and Information Sciences, launched an M.S. in Applied AI and IoT in fall 2025, and debuted a B.S. in Business Analytics with AI the same fall. According to [FIU Business](https://business.fiu.edu/business-now/2026/spring-2026/built-for-the-ai-economy.html), that business program hit capacity less than a year after launch.
- **University of Miami** runs the [Frost Institute for Data Science and Computing](https://idsc.miami.edu/about/), funded in part by a $100 million gift from Phillip and Patricia Frost. Its work spans AI and machine learning, digital health, earth systems and smart cities, and it's hosting an AI hackathon weekend on November 7–8, 2026.

!! 2024: the year Miami Dade College launched Florida's first bachelor's degree in applied AI

## What are the city and county doing about AI?

Government is moving carefully, but it is moving. In July 2023, the Miami-Dade Board of County Commissioners adopted a resolution directing the county to build an AI policy. The result is a [county AI framework](https://www.miamidade.gov/global/comms-tech/technology/ai/home.page), a 2025 AI report and an employee policy that limits staff to county-approved generative AI tools, requires human review of AI output, asks for disclosure in public-facing documents and makes AI training mandatory.

The City of Miami, for its part, used a $250,000 Google donation for a three-month pilot with C3.ai to speed up zoning verification letters, as [GovTech reported](https://insider.govtech.com/florida/news/miami-turns-to-ai-to-produce-zoning-verification-letters). It's unglamorous paperwork, which is exactly where AI tends to pay off first.

## How is AI changing hospitality, real estate and media in Miami?

**Hospitality.** The quiet revolution is in messaging and the booking journey. Guest-messaging vendor Metaguest.AI [announced a 29-hotel expansion in Miami](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/207544/Metaguest.AI-Announces-Significant-Hotel-Partner-Expansion-in-Miami-Florida), offering a virtual concierge, mobile checkout and in-room controls. The bigger strategic question for Miami hoteliers, as Fortune argued in May 2026, is visibility: if travelers ask an AI assistant where to stay and your hotel doesn't come up, it may as well not exist.

**Real estate.** The [MIAMI Association of Realtors partnered with Ethica AI](https://salestechstar.com/partner-management-channel-enablement/miami-association-of-realtors-and-ethica-ai-partner-to-help-miami-members-serve-clients-better/) to give members AI marketing tools for their listings. Nationally, a 2026 Delta Media Group survey reported by [HousingWire](https://housingwire.com/articles/real-estate-brokerage-ai-adoption-hits-a-tipping-point-as-holdouts-disappear) found 97% of brokerage leaders say their agents use AI. Expect sharper listing copy, faster comps and virtual staging, and expect the human broker with the Fisher Island phone list to remain irreplaceable.

**Media.** Miami's role as the capital of Spanish-language media is getting an AI layer. In September 2026, Entravision announced AHORA, a national Spanish-language news streaming brand that originates from a new Miami operations hub, uses Unreal Engine virtual production and AI-assisted workflows, and introduces an AI "information companion" called LIA.

**Mobility.** Overhead, the future is penciled in. In December 2025, [Archer Aviation laid out plans](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251203175518/en/Archer-Reveals-Plans-for-Miami-Air-Taxi-Network-Featuring-Partnerships-With-Related-Ross-and-Magic-City-Innovation-District) for a South Florida air-taxi network linking MIA, FLL and PBI, with a vertiport at the Magic City Innovation District in Little Haiti and electric operations from Hard Rock Stadium's helipad. It still needs FAA certification of its Midnight aircraft first.

## What to watch before March 2027

1. **Whether AI dollars hold.** The 2025 jump in AI-focused funding sets a high bar for 2026 totals.
2. **Talent pipelines.** Graduates from MDC's and FIU's newer AI programs start hitting the job market.
3. **eMerge's earlier date.** A March conference changes the rhythm of Miami's spring season, and the side-event map will follow.
4. **Public-sector AI.** The county's staged rollout moves from training and governance toward deployed tools.

## Through the lens

Miami tech is hard to photograph because so much of it happens on screens. Shoot the edges instead. During eMerge week, the Miami Beach Convention Center at golden hour, with badges swinging and the Collins Avenue palms behind, tells the story better than any keynote stage. In Brickell and Wynwood, look for the after-hours light of coworking floors against the skyline; a long lens from a parking-garage roof turns a lit office into a diorama. At Little Haiti's Magic City Innovation District, frame the murals and warehouses that may one day sit beside a vertiport. And if you want people, go to the college events: MDC and FIU hackathons are full of faces that will be running this city's companies in ten years. Ask before you shoot, and keep laptop screens out of focus. Some of that code is somebody's next round.
