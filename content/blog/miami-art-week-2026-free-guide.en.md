It starts with a sound: the hollow *tok* of a ping-pong ball on a table that is also a sculpture, under the palms of Collins Park, a block from the Atlantic. Fifty yards away, collectors with lanyards hurry toward the Convention Center. Nobody at the table has paid a cent. That, in one frame, is the quiet secret of Miami Art Week.

**The short answer:** Miami Art Week 2026 runs roughly November 30 to December 6, and [Art Basel Miami Beach](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-basel-announces-exhibitors-and-a-reconfigured-showfloor-for-miami-beach-2026) opens to the public December 4–6 at the Miami Beach Convention Center (VIP days are December 2–3). The fair itself needs a ticket, but a surprising amount of the week costs nothing: public art in Collins Park, ICA Miami's always-free galleries, the Design District's outdoor commissions and Wynwood's mural streets. Plan by neighborhood, not by party invite, and you will see more art than most VIPs.

!! 272 exhibitors: the size of Art Basel Miami Beach 2026, from more than 40 countries and territories, according to Art Basel.

## How did a Swiss fair end up on Miami Beach?

Art Basel was born in Switzerland in 1970, and its American edition was meant to debut in December 2001. Six weeks before opening, organizers [postponed it for a year](https://www.e-flux.com/announcements/43605/art-basel-miami-beach-postponed), citing the climate in the United States after September 11. The rescheduled first edition opened December 5–8, 2002, and the bet paid off: Miami Beach became the place where the art world closes its year, and the fair the gravitational center around which a whole week now orbits.

The scale has kept growing. According to an [EFE report published by Telemundo 51](https://www.telemundo51.com/noticias/local/art-basel-miami-beach-reunio-a-mas-de-80000-visitantes-y-43-paises/2727213/), the 2025 edition drew more than 80,000 visitors. Art Basel's own [2025 wrap-up](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-basel-miami-beach-closes-year-on-a-high-note) described strong sales and packed Conversations talks.

> Art Basel sells the art. Miami gives away the week.

## What's new at Art Basel Miami Beach 2026?

The headline is the floor plan. Art Basel says it has [reconfigured the showfloor](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/art-basel-announces-exhibitors-and-a-reconfigured-showfloor-for-miami-beach-2026), scattering blue-chip galleries across the hall instead of clustering them in one prestige zone, a change that rewards wandering. The core Galleries sector counts 204 exhibitors, and more than 30 galleries are first-timers, most of them in the project-based Nova, Positions and Survey sectors.

Then there is Zero 10, Art Basel's platform for art of the digital era, returning with 22 galleries and platforms. If you have only one paid day, this is the corner where the fair feels most like a preview of what is coming rather than a summary of what already sold.

!! 2002: the year Art Basel Miami Beach debuted, after a postponement following September 11, 2001.

## What can you see for free during Miami Art Week 2026?

Here is the short list, organized so you can actually walk it.

| Spot | Neighborhood | Cost | Why go |
|---|---|---|---|
| *Between Tides*, Collins Park | Miami Beach (21st St) | Free | Artist-designed ping-pong tables by Public Art Fund with The Bass, Nov 29–Dec 6 |
| ICA Miami + sculpture garden | Design District | Free, every day | Serious contemporary programming, no ticket |
| Fly's Eye Dome, Palm Court | Design District | Free | Buckminster Fuller's 24-foot dome, open to the street |
| Wynwood streets | Wynwood | Free | Murals on nearly every wall outside the museum |
| Wynwood Walls | Wynwood | About $12 adult | The curated outdoor museum, with resident discounts |
| PAMM | Downtown / Museum Park | Free second Saturdays | Dec 12 falls right after Art Week |

### Collins Park: the people's Basel

The best free ticket of the week is a paddle. [Public Art Fund](https://www.publicartfund.org/exhibitions/view/between-tides/) brings *Between Tides*, a participatory exhibition of artist-designed ping-pong tables, to Collins Park, presented with The Bass. The [Greater Miami Convention & Visitors Bureau listing](https://www.miamiandbeaches.com/event/between-tides/43614) runs it November 29 through December 6, free and outdoors, with a "Paddle in the Park Classic" tournament on November 30. The show first played Rockaway Beach in New York this summer; whether the Miami tables are the same lineup is worth checking on arrival.

Tip: Collins Park is a short walk from the Convention Center. Pair it with an afternoon at the fair, or skip the fair and simply watch the week walk past.

### Design District: a free museum and a dome you can climb

[ICA Miami](https://www.wlrn.org/post/institute-contemporary-art-miami-now-open-new-home) opened its permanent home at 61 NE 41st Street in 2017, and admission is free. Its sculpture garden is one of the calmest places in the city during the busiest week of the year.

A few blocks away, in Palm Court (140 NE 39th Street), sits Buckminster Fuller's [Fly's Eye Dome](https://www.miamidesigndistrict.com/listing/buckminster-fuller-flys-eye-dome/), a 24-foot reproduction installed in 2014 of a design Fuller patented in 1965 as a prototype for low-cost housing. It is photogenic from every angle, and nobody will ask for a wristband.

### Wynwood: pay for the Walls, or don't

The [Wynwood Walls](https://thewynwoodwalls.com/faqs/) is a ticketed outdoor museum (listed hours 10:30 a.m. to 6:30 p.m.), with general admission around $12 on major ticket platforms and discounts for Miami-Dade residents, students, teachers, military and first responders with ID. Worth it once. But the neighborhood around it is the real show, and the streets cost nothing.

## Which satellite fairs should be on your list?

Miami Art Week is not one fair but a constellation. Published calendars list these 2026 dates; confirm hours and tickets on each fair's own site before you go.

- **Untitled Art** – Dec 2–6, in a tent on the sand at Ocean Drive and 12th Street. Emerging and mid-career galleries, ocean breeze included.
- **NADA Miami** – Dec 1–5, Ice Palace Studios, Miami. Young galleries, often the best discoveries per square foot.
- **Design Miami** – Dec 1–6, next to the Convention Center. Collectible design, from mid-century to commissions.
- **Art Miami + CONTEXT** – Dec 1–6, One Herald Plaza, on the bay in downtown Miami.
- **Aqua Art Miami** – Dec 2–6, Miami Beach. Compact, approachable, often the friendliest price points.

Most charge admission, but their prices are generally below Art Basel's, and several are walkable from one another.

## How should you plan Miami Art Week by neighborhood?

Traffic over the causeways during Art Week is legendary, and not in a good way. Treat the city like three villages.

1. **Miami Beach day.** Art Basel or Design Miami in the morning, Collins Park and *Between Tides* at lunch, Untitled on Ocean Drive for the late afternoon light.
2. **Design District + Wynwood day.** ICA Miami when it opens, the Fly's Eye Dome and the district's public art, then Wynwood's streets and NADA.
3. **Downtown day.** Art Miami + CONTEXT on the bay, Museum Park and PAMM, the Metromover (free) as your shuttle.

Book nothing in two villages on the same evening. Bikes, scooters and walking beat a car most hours of the week.

## Through the lens

**For photographers:** Collins Park works best at golden hour, when the ping-pong tables throw long shadows and players turn into silhouettes against The Bass. Shoot low and wide. In the Design District, the Fly's Eye Dome is a gift for symmetry: stand dead center under a circular opening and let the geometry frame the sky. In Wynwood, go early, before 9 a.m., when the light is soft and the sidewalks empty; by noon, every mural has a crowd.

**For everyone else:** wear walking shoes, carry water, and keep one evening free. The most Miami moment of the week is rarely the one on the invitation; it is the stranger who hands you a paddle in Collins Park.
