## 6. Fair use and how we apply it

U.S. law permits limited use of copyrighted material without permission for purposes such as criticism, comment, news reporting, teaching and research. Courts weigh four factors: the purpose and character of the use, the nature of the work, the amount used, and the effect on the market for the original ([17 U.S.C. §107](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/107)). Fair use is decided case by case.

MIAGAZINE relies on fair use narrowly:

- **Quotations:** short, attributed passages when we review, comment on or report about a book, film, show, article or statement.
- **Logos:** brand logos only to identify the company or event we are reporting on.
- **Screenshots and frames:** of social media posts, websites or videos when the content itself is the news or the object of commentary, with the source identified.
- **Artwork and installations:** images of art in exhibitions or public spaces, in connection with coverage of that exhibition or artist, and credited whenever possible.

We do not use third-party photographs as decoration, nor use "fair use" to avoid licensing images we could reasonably license. If you believe a specific use exceeds fair use, contact us (section 8).

## 7. Permissions and releases

### 7.1 People in public spaces

In the United States, photographing people who are visible in public spaces for editorial and news use generally does not require consent. MIAGAZINE publishes such images to inform and document Miami life — not to sell products.

### 7.2 Name and likeness (Florida)

Florida prohibits publishing, printing or otherwise using a person's name, portrait, photograph or likeness for trade, commercial or advertising purposes without that person's express written or oral consent ([Fla. Stat. §540.08](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0540/Sections/0540.08.html)). The statute exempts, among other things, publication in a newspaper, magazine, book or news broadcast as part of a bona fide news report or presentation having a current and legitimate public interest, where the likeness is not used for advertising.

Our policy is stricter than the statute: **any commercial use — advertising, Partner content, brand campaigns, merchandise — requires the person's written consent (a model release).** We do not use editorial photos of identifiable people in sponsored content without one.

### 7.3 Model, property and location releases

- **Model release:** required for posed shoots, editorials featuring identifiable models, and any commercial use.
- **Property / location release:** required to photograph inside private homes, businesses, venues and private property, or where the owner restricts photography. Contributors must keep releases and provide them on request.
- **Artwork and design:** recognizable copyrighted artwork shown prominently may require the artist's permission for non-editorial uses.

### 7.4 Minors

The site is not directed to children under 13 (see the Children's Online Privacy Protection Act, [15 U.S.C. §6501 et seq.](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/chapter-91)). We publish identifiable photos of minors only with written consent from a parent or legal guardian, except incidental images in crowds at public news events. We will remove a child's image upon a parent's or guardian's request.

### 7.5 Private places and privacy

We never photograph or record people where they have a reasonable expectation of privacy — restrooms, changing rooms, homes viewed through windows, and similar places. Florida criminalizes secretly viewing, recording or broadcasting a person in such circumstances ([Fla. Stat. §810.145](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0800-0899/0810/Sections/0810.145.html), video voyeurism). We do not accept submissions made with hidden cameras or in breach of privacy.

### 7.6 Event photography notice

Attendees at public events that MIAGAZINE covers may be photographed or filmed, and those images may appear on our site, newsletter and social media. Where possible we post signage or announce our presence. If you do not wish to be photographed, tell our photographer at the event. If an image of you has been published and you want it removed or blurred, email corrections@miagazine.com with the link and a short description; we review requests promptly and generally honor them for non-newsworthy images of private individuals.

### 7.7 Filming permits and drones

Commercial and some editorial productions in public spaces may require a permit from the relevant local office — for example, the City of Miami, City of Miami Beach and Miami-Dade County film offices. MIAGAZINE and its contributors obtain permits when required. Drone (small unmanned aircraft) operations for publication are commercial and must comply with FAA rules ([14 CFR Part 107](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-F/part-107)), including a certified remote pilot and airspace authorizations. We do not publish drone footage obtained in violation of those rules.

### 8.2 What your notice must include — 17 U.S.C. §512(c)(3)

1. A physical or electronic signature of the copyright owner or a person authorized to act on their behalf.
2. Identification of the copyrighted work claimed to be infringed (or a representative list if there are several).
3. Identification of the material claimed to be infringing and information reasonably sufficient for us to locate it (the exact URL).
4. Your contact information: name, address, telephone number and email.
5. A statement that you have a good-faith belief that the use is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorized to act on behalf of the owner.

Incomplete notices may not be effective. Knowingly misrepresenting that material is infringing can create liability for damages ([17 U.S.C. §512(f)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512)). Please consider fair use before filing.

### 8.3 Counter-notice — 17 U.S.C. §512(g)

If your material was removed and you believe this was a mistake or misidentification, send a counter-notice to the agent including: your signature; identification of the material and where it appeared before removal; a statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good-faith belief it was removed by mistake or misidentification; and your name, address and telephone number, with consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for your address (or, if outside the U.S., any judicial district where MIAGAZINE may be found) and agreement to accept service from the person who sent the original notice. We will forward it to the complainant and may restore the material in 10 to 14 business days unless we are notified that a court action has been filed.

### 8.4 Repeat infringers

Consistent with [17 U.S.C. §512(i)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512), we will, in appropriate circumstances, terminate the submission privileges and accounts of contributors or users who repeatedly infringe.
