Seven a.m. on a Brickell rooftop. Nobody is wearing a logo. A woman in a plain linen set steps out of a cold plunge, checks her heart-rate variability on a ring, and books a sunset sail for twelve. On her wrist is a twenty-year-old steel watch bought pre-owned; downstairs, the residence she owns carries the name of a hotel brand. Every element of that scene is a luxury trend, and none of it involves a shopping bag.

That's the headline for 2027: luxury is being redefined from *what you own* to *how you live*. Spending on goods is growing slowly after a rough 2025, while experiences, wellness, resale and residences are pulling ahead. Brands are also leaning on AI to make service feel personal again. Miami, with its new wealth, year-round outdoor life and appetite for display, is where many of these shifts show up first in the Americas.

## Where does the luxury market stand going into 2027?

Stabilizing, not booming. According to the spring 2026 update of the [Bain & Company–Altagamma luxury study](https://www.zawya.com/en/press-release/research-and-studies/global-luxury-stabilizes-amid-compounding-disruptions-as-brands-race-to-amplify-meaning-and-rebuild-relevance-e05i9bvr), worldwide luxury spending (goods, cars, hospitality, dining, yachts and more) reached about €1.443 trillion in 2025 and is expected to land between €1.44 and €1.47 trillion in 2026, roughly flat to up 2%.

Personal luxury goods (fashion, leather, jewelry, watches, beauty) shrank to €358 billion in 2025 from €364 billion the year before. Bain forecasts 2–4% growth in 2026, to €365–373 billion. Jewelry is the strongest category; by region, the Americas are accelerating while Europe and the Middle East hold back.

!! €358 billion: the size of the personal luxury goods market in 2025, down from €364 billion in 2024 (Bain–Altagamma, 2026).

The tone from [McKinsey and The Business of Fashion's State of Fashion 2026](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/apparel-fashion-and-luxury/our-insights/state-of-fashion) is similarly sober: the era of easy, price-led growth in luxury is fading, and 46% of executives surveyed expected industry conditions to worsen in 2026. Translation: buyers have noticed the price hikes, and they want something more than a bag that costs 50% more than it did in 2019.

> The new status symbol isn't the thing. It's the time, the access and the body to enjoy it.

## Quiet luxury or maximalism: which wins?

Neither, completely. "Quiet luxury" (logo-free cashmere, neutral palettes, the old-money look) was the defining mood of 2023–2024. It hasn't vanished, but the more durable trend is **selectivity**: fewer purchases, chosen carefully, held longer.

In Miami, that plays out as a split personality. By day: unstructured linen, minimalist sandals, a watch you have to know to recognize. By night: Wynwood color, statement jewelry, vintage Versace. Jewelry being Bain's strongest category fits this perfectly: it can be quiet or loud, and it holds value.

What *is* fading is the logo-as-receipt. When price no longer guarantees exclusivity, taste does the signaling.

## Why are experiences beating products?

Bain's 2026 update found demand for luxury experiences growing roughly **1.5 times faster** than demand for tangible goods. Think private dining, yacht charters, hotel buyouts, art-week access and members' clubs.

Miami is built for this. The city's luxury calendar (Art Basel Miami Beach in December, the boat show in February, Swim Week in late May, Formula 1 in spring) sells moments, not merchandise. Hotel brands now program cultural events the way galleries do, and restaurants double as venues.

For 2027, expect the line between hospitality and retail to blur further: brand-hosted dinners, invitation-only previews and "access" sold as the product itself.

## Is longevity the new luxury?

All signs say yes. The [Global Wellness Institute](https://globalwellnessinstitute.org/industry-research/2025-global-wellness-economy-monitor/) puts the global wellness economy at **$6.8 trillion in 2024**, up 7.9% in a year, with North America (about $2.3 trillion) now the largest regional market. GWI projects $9.8 trillion by 2029.

!! $6.8 trillion: the global wellness economy in 2024, larger than tourism or IT spending, per the Global Wellness Institute (2025).

Luxury's version of this is the **longevity club**: part gym, part clinic, part social club, with diagnostics, recovery suites, cold plunges, infrared saunas and physician oversight. Miami has seen a wave of announced and opened projects, from New York-born wellness clubs adding Miami outposts to hotel members' clubs and Soho House's plan to turn its Miami pool house into a health club. McKinsey's report flags the same shift from the consumer side: aspirational luxury buyers are redirecting spending toward wellbeing.

The 2027 question isn't whether wellness is luxury. It's which offers hold up to scrutiny. Expect members to ask harder questions about medical credentials and evidence.

## Resale: why is pre-owned now a luxury channel?

Because the math works for everyone. [BCG and Vestiaire Collective](https://www.bcg.com/press/9october2025-new-report-from-bcg-and-vestiaire-collective-reveals-global-trends-reshaping-the-resale-market) estimated in October 2025 that the secondhand fashion and luxury market is worth $210–220 billion, growing about three times faster than the firsthand market, and could reach $320–360 billion by 2030. Secondhand already accounts for roughly 8% of fashion and luxury sales.

!! Up to $360 billion: the projected size of the secondhand fashion and luxury market by 2030 (BCG and Vestiaire Collective, 2025).

Two categories lead in Miami:

- **Watches.** After the post-2022 correction, secondary prices for the big Swiss names have largely stopped falling, according to industry trackers. For buyers frustrated by waitlists and list-price hikes, certified pre-owned is now the default first stop, and Miami's dealer scene (from the Seybold Building downtown to boutiques in the Design District) is one of the busiest in the country.
- **Handbags.** Vintage and pre-owned bags have become part of the luxury closet rather than a compromise, especially as new-bag prices climbed.

For 2027: expect more brands to run their own certified resale programs, and more authentication tech built into the purchase.

## Why does Miami keep building branded residences?

Because buyers pay for the name, and the service that comes with it. [Savills' 2025/26 branded residences report](https://pdf.savills.com/documents/Savills-Branded-Residences-Annual-Report-2025-2026.pdf) projected the number of branded schemes worldwide would rise from 764 at the end of 2024 to 910 by the end of 2025, a 19% jump, with South Florida leading the Americas.

The model is evolving beyond hotel brands. Fashion houses, automakers and even wellness concepts now attach their names to towers, and Miami's skyline is a showroom for the idea. Amenity lists increasingly read like club brochures: longevity suites, private chefs, owners-only beach clubs.

## How is AI changing luxury retail?

Quietly, and mostly behind the counter. The [BCG–Altagamma True-Luxury Global Consumer Insights 2026](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/luxury-healthier-foundations-and-genai) study, based on more than 10,000 respondents, found that **87% of luxury consumers use generative AI weekly** and about 40% daily. In [Deloitte's Global Powers of Luxury Goods 2026](https://www.deloitte.com/content/dam/assets-shared/docs/industries/consumer/2026/global-powers-of-luxury-goods-2026.pdf), a survey of 420 senior executives, demand for hyper-personalization and data-enabled services ranked as the top consumer trend.

In practice, that means sales associates who remember everything (your sizes, your partner's birthday, the watch you tried on last year), product discovery that starts in a chatbot rather than a search bar, and concierge services that blend human and machine. Bain itself lists AI-enabled personalization among the three priorities for brands, alongside immersive experiences and cultural relevance.

The risk for 2027 is obvious: luxury runs on human warmth. The brands that win will use AI to make their people more attentive, not to replace them.

## Through the lens

How to see (and photograph) 2027's luxury in Miami:

- **Shoot rituals, not products.** A sunrise cold plunge, a sommelier mid-pour, hands adjusting a vintage watch: experiences photograph better than shelves.
- **Go early in the Design District.** Before 10 a.m., the architecture and window displays are empty and the light is clean.
- **Follow the cranes.** Branded-residence sales galleries are open by appointment and are some of the best-designed interiors in the city.
- **Mind the rules.** Private clubs, spas and residences almost always prohibit photography of members; ask before you shoot, and get a property release for commercial use.
- **For readers:** if you want to test the trend without the initiation fee, many hotel spas and recovery studios offer day passes, and the best pre-owned dealers will let you try on history for free.
