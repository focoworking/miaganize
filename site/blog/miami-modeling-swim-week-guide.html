Miami Modeling Guide: Swim Week, Agencies and Staying Safe

From poolside runways to licensed agencies: how Miami's model industry really works, what Swim Week 2027 may bring, and how to spot a scam.

The runway is a pool. A model steps onto a plexiglass catwalk floating over turquoise water, the Atlantic hums a block away, and two hundred phones rise in unison. Somewhere in the crowd a buyer from Milan is taking notes, and somewhere behind the velvet rope a nineteen-year-old from Hialeah is wondering how you get from watching this to walking it.

Here's the short answer: Miami is one of the world's real modeling markets, not just a backdrop. It is the global stage for swim and resort fashion every late spring, it hosts a growing fall fashion week, and several international agencies keep Miami boards. Breaking in works the same way it does in New York or Paris (through a licensed agency that earns a commission, never through upfront fees), and Florida law backs you up on that.

Why Miami, of all places?

Light, mostly. The city's year-round sun, beaches and Art Deco streets made it a catalog and campaign location decades before anyone called it a fashion capital. Shoots come for the weather; agencies followed the shoots. Then swimwear found its natural home here, and the city became the place where the industry previews next summer's collections a full year ahead.

In Miami, the season you see on the runway is never the season you're standing in.

Miami Swim Week 2026: what happened

The anchor is Paraiso Miami Swim Week, which held its 22nd edition from May 28 to 31, 2026, in Miami Beach, according to the Greater Miami Convention & Visitors Bureau listing. Collections were shown for spring, resort and cruise 2027.

FashionUnited's recap and other trade coverage highlighted a few moments:

  • Luli Fama marked its 20th anniversary with one of the week's biggest shows, a reminder that homegrown Miami labels still set the tone.
  • Megan Thee Stallion brought her swim line to the runway, which dominated social feeds.
  • Kulani Kinis, the Australian label, showed for the first time, while Oh Polly returned for a fifth year.
  • RISE, a new platform for emerging designers, staged shows over the pool at the Kimpton Surfcomber.
60+ countriesbuyers, press and industry members from more than 60 nations attended Paraiso in 2026, per trade reports.

Paraiso isn't the only producer that week. Miami Swim Week – The Shows, produced by DCSW Group, ran its 2026 program around the Mondrian South Beach, which means the last week of May now looks like a citywide festival with overlapping calendars. For a new model, that's opportunity, but also noise: dozens of shows, castings and "activations," not all of them equal.

What can we expect from Swim Week 2027?

As of today (November 1, 2026), Paraiso has not published its 2027 dates; its official listing simply notes a new date will be posted when available. Based on the event's long-standing pattern, a late-May window is the reasonable bet, but don't book flights on a guess.

Some educated expectations, based on 2026's direction rather than any announcement:

  • More emerging-designer programming. RISE was introduced in 2026 specifically for newer brands; platforms like that tend to grow in their second year.
  • Celebrity-led labels will keep drawing the cameras.
  • Poolside, immersive staging remains Paraiso's signature, so expect water, daylight and very little traditional black-box runway.

And Miami Fashion Week?

Miami's fall counterpart, Miami Fashion Week, celebrated its 10th anniversary edition (counted since its relaunch) in October 2026. FashionUnited's event listing and the official site gave dates of October 13–17, with an opening gala at Queen Miami Beach on October 13. Where Swim Week is about bikinis and resort, MIAFW leans toward Latin American and Ibero-American designers, ready-to-wear and evening, a different casting profile and a different crowd.

Which agencies have Miami offices?

Several international agencies maintain Miami boards, according to the industry directory Models.com and agency records:

  • Elite Miami: part of the Elite network with offices in New York, Los Angeles, Miami and Toronto.
  • Next Miami: the Miami arm of NEXT Management.
  • Ford Models Miami: listed by Models.com with a Brickell Key address.
  • Wilhelmina Miami: moved from its longtime home at the Mondrian in Miami Beach to Wynwood in 2023, per The Real Deal.

Addresses change, so always confirm on each agency's official website, and remember that a famous name on a social media DM is not the same as a booker at that agency. Scammers impersonate big agencies precisely because the names open doors.

How do public events and runways actually work?

Most of what the public sees is the end of a long funnel. Brands book models through agencies weeks in advance; castings are held at agency offices or hotel suites; fittings happen the day before or the morning of; then hair, makeup and a run-through. Some shows are ticketed and open to the public, which is great for watching, but a ticket is not an audition.

Some independent shows recruit models directly, often for "exposure" rather than pay. That isn't automatically a scam, but get the terms in writing: who owns the photos, whether you'll be paid, and whether you're expected to buy the swimsuit (you shouldn't be).

How do new models build a book safely?

A "book" is your portfolio: a handful of strong, simple images that show your face, body and range. Agencies prefer natural digitals (clean, unretouched phone or camera shots in fitted basics) over expensive glam. Plenty of signed models started with nothing more.

Red flags the FTC and advocates warn about

The FTC's guidance on modeling scams is blunt: an agency that asks you to pay up front to represent you is a scam signal. The Model Alliance adds that scam outfits often demand upfront payments to "represent" aspiring models. Watch for:

  1. A fee to join, register or "be in our book."
  2. Pressure to use (and pay) one specific photographer.
  3. Guaranteed jobs or big-income promises.
  4. Cash, wire, crypto or gift-card payments only.
  5. A scout in a mall or a DM that quickly moves you to WhatsApp.
  6. "Sign today or lose your spot."

If something feels off, you can report it to the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov or to the Florida Attorney General's fraud line.

What does Florida law say?

Florida is unusually specific. Under Chapter 468, Part VII of the Florida Statutes (read it on the Florida Senate site), no one may operate a talent agency in the state without a license from the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). The DBPR application involves background screening, and licenses must be renewed.

Two provisions every model should know:

  • Section 468.410 prohibits talent agencies from charging registration fees.
  • Section 468.412 says that if an agency collects a fee or expenses to get you work and you don't get the job (or don't get paid), it must refund on demand, and if it doesn't within 48 hours, it owes an additional amount equal to the fee.
48 hoursthe window a Florida talent agency has to refund a fee for a job that fell through before it owes you an additional sum equal to the fee (Fla. Stat. 468.412, as of 2025).

Before signing, look the agency up on the DBPR's license search at myfloridalicense.com, read the contract (term, commission, exclusivity, who pays for test shoots), and if you're under 18, have a parent or guardian review everything. For anything complex, a quick consultation with an entertainment lawyer is cheap insurance.

What should photographers know?

Miami is crowded with photographers during Swim Week, and many shoot models for portfolios, test shoots and personal work. The legal rule of thumb from ASMP: get a signed model release for any commercial use of a recognizable person, and ASMP counts self-promotion of your photography business as commercial use. Editorial use generally doesn't require one, but images travel, and a release protects both sides.

Practical habits:

  • Use a separate minor release signed by a parent or guardian for anyone under 18.
  • On TFP (time for prints) shoots, put usage rights in writing: who can post, sell or license what.
  • Hotels and private venues may require a property release or permit; Miami Beach's public beaches also have rules for commercial shoots, so check permits before a big production.
  • Credit the model and agency when posting; it's good etiquette and good business.

Through the lens

Want to experience (or photograph) Miami's modeling world without getting played?

  • Shoot the edges. The best Swim Week images rarely come from the runway itself; they're backstage, at fittings, or in the 6 p.m. light along Collins Avenue between shows.
  • Arrive early, leave late. Morning pool light at the Surfcomber or the Mondrian is softer than anything at showtime.
  • For models: bring a printed comp card, fresh digitals on your phone and a neutral swimsuit. Walk into agency open calls, not hotel rooms.
  • For readers: many shows sell tickets; buy from official channels and treat "VIP casting" upsells as the red flag they usually are.
  • Watch the calendar. Swim Week in late May, Miami Fashion Week in October, and Art Basel in December all draw fashion crowds. The city is a runway three seasons a year.

Questions people ask

When is Miami Swim Week 2027?

As of November 2026, Paraiso has not announced its 2027 dates. The 2026 edition ran May 28–31 in Miami Beach, and Swim Week traditionally lands in late May or early June, so plan around that window and confirm on official channels before booking travel.

Do modeling agencies in Florida need a license?

Yes. Florida Statutes Chapter 468, Part VII requires anyone operating a talent agency in the state to be licensed by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR). You can check an agency's status through the DBPR's online license search before you sign anything.

Is it normal to pay a modeling agency to sign with them?

No. The FTC warns that an agency asking for money up front to represent you is a red flag, and Florida law prohibits talent agencies from charging registration fees. Legitimate agencies earn a commission on the work they book for you.

Do photographers need a model release in Miami?

For commercial use, yes. ASMP advises getting a signed release for any commercial use of a recognizable person, including self-promotion. Editorial use generally doesn't require one, but a release protects everyone if the image later travels.
