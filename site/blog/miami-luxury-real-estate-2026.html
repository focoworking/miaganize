Miami Luxury Real Estate 2026: Logos in the Sky, Cracks Below

Supercar towers, a 1,049-foot Waldorf and a $4.3M entry ticket to the top 5%. Meanwhile, Miami's older condos are paying for Surfside. Here's the 2026 market.

By MIAGAZINE Editorial

In 30 seconds
  • Luxury keeps getting pricier: entry to Miami-Dade's top 5% of single-family sales rose to $4.3 million in the first half of 2026, up from $3.2 million in 2025 (MIAMI REALTORS).
  • The market has split: new and branded towers sell at a premium while condos 30+ years old absorb the costs of Florida's post-Surfside safety laws (SB 4-D, HB 913).
  • Buyers come from New York and Latin America: foreign buyers spent $4.4 billion in South Florida in 2025, led by Colombia and Argentina, and Miami-Dade took 73% of them.

From the deck of a boat on Biscayne Bay, Miami's skyline now reads like a car show and a hotel lobby stacked together. Aston Martin's curved sail rises at the mouth of the river. Behind it, a column of offset cubes keeps climbing toward 1,049 feet: the Waldorf Astoria. Up in Sunny Isles, Bentley is building a tower with car elevators. The logos have moved off the hoods and onto the skyline.

Short answer: in 2026, Miami luxury real estate is still rising, but not all of it. New and branded product sells at a premium, and the price to get into the top tier keeps climbing. Condos built decades ago are a different market. They are paying for Florida's post-Surfside safety laws through higher dues, special assessments and softer prices. In practice, Miami now runs two luxury markets, and the age of the building decides which one you're in.

How expensive is "luxury" in Miami now?

The bar keeps moving. According to the MIAMI REALTORS Q2 2026 luxury report, the price that puts a Miami-Dade single-family sale in the top 5% rose to $4.3 million in the first half of 2026, from $3.2 million in 2025. Joining the top 1% now takes $15 million, up from $10.4 million.

$4.3 millionthe entry price to Miami-Dade's top 5% of single-family sales in H1 2026 (MIAMI REALTORS)

The rise wasn't one sudden jump. The association's Q1 2026 report had already put the top-5% line at $4.1 million and the top-1% line at $13.6 million. So the threshold rose quarter after quarter.

Across the wider market, the trends point in opposite directions. MIAMI REALTORS data for June 2026 showed the countywide single-family median up about 3.7% year over year to $695,000, while the condo median fell about 3.2% to $431,000. Houses are getting more expensive and condos are getting cheaper. Branded towers are the exception: they are condos that sell on the strength of the brand.

Why is every luxury brand building a tower in Miami?

Miami didn't invent branded residences, but it made automotive branding famous. Porsche Design Tower in Sunny Isles Beach, completed in 2017 by Dezer Development, was the world's first car-branded residential tower. Its car elevators delivered supercars to the living room, and that image went around the world.

Now the idea has spread across the skyline:

The Aston Martin tower shows how the model works. The Real Deal reported that the building was about 99% presold at completion. Its Sky Penthouse later closed at roughly $24 million, which Real Deal and Profile Miami described as a downtown Miami condo record.

The Waldorf will be the signature. Florida YIMBY reported in February 2026 that the superstructure had passed the 60th floor, rising about one floor every ten days. Top-out is targeted for late 2026 and completion for January 2028. It will be Miami's first supertall: a 100-story landmark that will change the city's skyline.

In Miami, a brand isn't decoration anymore. Buyers treat it like a warranty: a promise of service, resale value and a building that won't surprise them.

Why does it work? Branded towers promise hotel-level service, design with a signature and, increasingly, peace of mind. A new building comes with modern structure and fully funded reserves, and in post-Surfside Florida buyers are willing to pay for that.

What did Surfside change for Miami condos?

Almost everything. After Champlain Towers South collapsed in June 2021, the Legislature passed SB 4-D in 2022. As the Florida Bar Journal explains, the law created two obligations for condo buildings of three or more stories:

  • Milestone inspections: structural inspections by an engineer or architect once a building reaches a certain age, then every ten years.
  • Structural Integrity Reserve Studies (SIRS): studies that set how much each association must save for roofs, structure, waterproofing, plumbing and other critical items. Associations can no longer vote to waive reserves for those items, a ban in force since December 31, 2024.

In 2025, HB 913 loosened some of the rules. As summarized by the law firm Lowndes, it was signed in June 2025 and took effect July 1. It pushed the first SIRS deadline to December 31, 2025, clarified which buildings count by "habitable stories," and allowed associations to fund required work with loans or lines of credit.

December 31, 2024the date Florida associations lost the ability to waive reserves for critical structural components

Who pays?

Owners of older buildings, mostly. Deferred maintenance that used to sit quietly in a budget now shows up as a bill. A study by ISG World, reported by Bisnow, found that average sale prices for older South Florida condos had fallen about 21% from 2023 levels, while condos under ten years old had gained about 9%. Reports of special assessments in the tens of thousands of dollars per unit, and sometimes six figures, are now common in local news.

For a luxury buyer, that changes the order of questions. The view still matters, but the questions about inspections and reserves now come first.

Who is buying Miami luxury in 2026?

Two groups, mainly.

Out-of-state buyers. According to MIAMI REALTORS (January 2026), domestic migration into Miami-Dade and Broward increased, with New York again the top feeder state. Finance moves (see Citadel's Brickell headquarters) and Florida's lack of a state income tax keep sending this wave south.

Latin Americans. The 2025 MIAMI International Report shows foreign buyers spent $4.4 billion on South Florida homes in 2025, up from $3.1 billion in 2024, which makes the region the top U.S. market for international buyers. Miami-Dade took 73% of those buyers. Colombia and Argentina led, together accounting for 27% of international closed sales.

$4.4 billionforeign-buyer spending on South Florida homes in 2025, up from $3.1 billion (MIAMI REALTORS)

Many of these buyers pay cash, which partly protects the top tier from mortgage rates. The squeeze falls on the middle of the market.

Is insurance still a dealbreaker?

It's less of one than two years ago. Florida's last-resort insurer, Citizens Property Insurance, shrank to about 255,000 policies by mid-September 2026, from roughly 1.4 million in late 2023, as private carriers took policies back, according to the News Service of Florida via Florida Trend. In March 2026 Citizens announced an average 8.7% rate decrease for most multiperil policyholders statewide, its first broad personal-lines cut since 2015.

That doesn't make coverage cheap. Waterfront, older and high-value properties still face steep premiums, and condo associations pass master-policy costs to owners through dues. But the 2023 sense of crisis has eased into a situation buyers can manage.

What should a luxury buyer check in 2026?

  • Building age and milestone status: request the latest milestone inspection report and see whether repairs are scheduled.
  • SIRS and reserves: check whether the association is fully funded or will need a special assessment to catch up.
  • Insurance: get the master policy cost and a quote for your unit before you make an offer.
  • Brand delivery: for pre-construction branded towers, read what services the brand actually commits to and for how long.
  • Exit liquidity: new branded product has held value well, but older condos in buildings with heavy assessments sit on the market longer.

Through the lens

For photographers: The best skyline frame of 2026 is from the water at golden hour, looking west at Aston Martin's sail and the Waldorf's cubes together. A tender or a bay cruise from Bayside works. On land, the Venetian Causeway gives a clean, low angle of the downtown towers with the bay in front. In Sunny Isles, shoot Porsche Design Tower and the rising Bentley tower from the beach at sunrise, when the glass turns pink. For cranes, photograph them now: a Miami skyline with tower cranes at work won't look like this for long.

For readers: Walk the Riverwalk from Brickell to the mouth of the Miami River to see the branded-tower wave up close. Book a drink at a Brickell rooftop to watch the Waldorf rise. And if you're house-hunting, bring an engineer's report to every condo showing, not just a tape measure. In Miami in 2026, the most important amenity is a building in good structural shape.

Questions people ask

Are Miami luxury home prices still going up in 2026?

Yes, at the top. MIAMI REALTORS reports the threshold for Miami-Dade's top 5% of single-family sales reached $4.3 million in the first half of 2026, versus $3.2 million in 2025, and the top 1% threshold rose to $15 million. Older condos are the exception, with prices under pressure from repair costs.

What are branded residences in Miami?

Condo towers designed and marketed under a luxury brand, from carmakers to hotels. Miami has Porsche Design Tower and Aston Martin Residences already built, with Bentley Residences, Waldorf Astoria, Cipriani and Mercedes-Benz Places under way. Buyers pay for design, services and brand prestige.

How does Florida's condo safety law affect buyers?

SB 4-D (2022) and HB 913 (2025) require milestone structural inspections and Structural Integrity Reserve Studies for buildings of three or more stories, and bar waiving reserves for key components. In older buildings that often means higher HOA dues or special assessments, so buyers should review reports and reserves before signing.

Who is buying luxury real estate in Miami?

Mostly domestic movers, led by New York, plus international buyers. MIAMI REALTORS reports foreign buyers spent $4.4 billion in South Florida in 2025, with Colombia and Argentina as the top two origins and Miami-Dade capturing 73% of foreign buyers.
