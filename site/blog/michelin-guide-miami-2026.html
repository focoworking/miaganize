Michelin in Miami 2026: The Stars, the Bibs and the Bargains

Fourteen starred dining rooms, a world-first kosher star and 18 Bib Gourmands. How Miami became Florida's Michelin capital, and how to eat it without going broke.

It is 9:40 on a Tuesday night in the Design District, and the counter at L'Atelier is running like a Swiss watch: black lacquer, red accents, cooks plating in total silence while the city honks outside. A few miles north, a kosher kitchen in North Miami is still absorbing the fact that it made world history this year. And somewhere in Little Havana, a line forms for a Cuban sandwich that Michelin inspectors also wrote down in their notebooks.

That is Miami's Michelin map in 2026, and it is wider than you think. According to the 2026 Michelin Guide Florida, Greater Miami and Miami Beach have 14 starred restaurants: one with two stars (L'Atelier de Joël Robuchon) and 13 with one, plus 18 Bib Gourmands. No other Florida destination comes close.

When did Michelin arrive in Miami?

The red guide landed on June 9, 2022, with an inaugural Florida edition that covered Miami, Orlando and Tampa. According to Michelin's 2022 announcement, that first selection listed 118 restaurants across 35 cuisine types, with 29 Bib Gourmands statewide. Miami took the lion's share of the stars: 10 one-star restaurants and the state's only two-star, L'Atelier de Joël Robuchon.

For a city long accused of valuing the scene over the plate, it was a turning point. Suddenly chefs had a reason to stay, and investors had a reason to bet on tasting menus instead of bottle service.

118 restaurantsthe size of Florida's first Michelin selection in 2022, covering only Miami, Orlando and Tampa.

What changed in the 2026 guide?

Everything got bigger. The 2026 edition, released digitally on May 28, 2026 after Michelin postponed its original April date and canceled the live ceremony (Miami New Times), is the first to cover the entire state. According to the official release, it recognizes 200 restaurants across 41 cuisine types, with 26 starred restaurants in Florida and only two at the two-star level: L'Atelier in Miami and Sorekara in Orlando.

The headline belongs to Miami. Mutra, in North Miami, earned one star and became, per Miami New Times, the first kosher restaurant in the world to hold a Michelin star. Chef Raz Shabtai's take on Middle Eastern cooking is exactly the kind of story Michelin likes: rooted, personal, impossible to franchise.

Miami also holds three Green Stars (Michelin's sustainability award), the most of any Florida destination, for Krüs Kitchen, Los Félix and Stubborn Seed, per the official release.

What defines Miami dining right now?

Look at that table again. Three patterns jump out.

Latin roots, fine-dining technique. Ariete, Elcielo and Los Félix prove that Miami's best cooking speaks Spanish first. Cuban, Colombian and Mexican flavors are no longer the "cheap eats" sidebar; they are the main event, with tasting menus and wine pairings.

The omakase boom. Hiden, Ogawa and Shingo are all Japanese counters where the chef decides and you surrender. Miami has become one of the most intense sushi cities in the country, fueled by fish flown in from Tokyo and a clientele happy to book a 10-seat room weeks ahead.

The steakhouse, reinvented. Cote's star shows that the city's beef obsession can be refined: Korean barbecue logic, dry-aged cuts, a cocktail list that belongs in a nightclub. Miami still loves a big night out. Now the big night out has a sommelier.

In Miami, the star used to be the DJ. Now it's the person holding the tweezers.

How do you eat Michelin-level food in Miami for less?

This is where the guide gets truly useful. The Bib Gourmand distinction flags restaurants with high-quality cooking at good value. According to Miami & Beaches and Michelin's own listings, Miami counts 18 Bib Gourmands in 2026, including four new ones: Barra Callao, Cotoa, Double Luck and To Be Determined.

18 Bib GourmandsMiami's count of Michelin's "great food, fair price" picks in 2026.

A short list to start with, based on the 2026 Bib Gourmand roundup:

  • Tâm Tâm (Downtown): Vietnamese, punchy and personal.
  • Sanguich De Miami (Little Havana): the Cuban sandwich, treated like a craft object.
  • El Turco (MiMo): Turkish grill with a neighborhood soul.
  • Phuc Yea (MiMo): Vietnamese-Cajun mashup, built for groups.
  • Chug's Diner (Coconut Grove): Cuban-American diner with real breakfast credibility.
  • Michael's Genuine (Design District): a Miami classic that keeps earning its place.
  • Zitz Sum (Coral Gables): dim sum with attitude.

More money-saving moves:

  1. Go at lunch. Several starred kitchens offer shorter menus at midday, and the Design District is calmer by daylight.
  2. Sit at the bar or counter. Walk-in seats and à la carte options exist at many top rooms; call the restaurant directly.
  3. Watch Miami Spice. The county's summer prix-fixe program (August and September) regularly includes guide-listed restaurants.
  4. Split the wine. Ask for half pours or a bottle from the lower end of the list. The sommelier won't judge; that's their job.

Is the South Beach Wine & Food Festival worth it?

If you want Miami's food scene in one weekend, yes. The 26th South Beach Wine & Food Festival runs February 25–28, 2027, in Miami Beach, according to Miami & Beaches. Expect walk-around tastings in tents on the sand, celebrity-chef dinners, burger and taco nights, and plenty of local chefs from the Michelin list cooking side by side with TV names.

Tips: buy early, since marquee events sell out; pick one big tasting plus one intimate dinner rather than five crowded tents; and use rideshare, because parking near the beach that weekend is a sport of its own.

Through the lens

Michelin food is made to be photographed, but the best images are rarely of the plate alone.

  • Shoot the counter, not just the course. At omakase rooms like Ogawa or Shingo, the chef's hands are the story. Ask permission, turn off the flash, and shoot at eye level with the counter.
  • Use Miami's light. Book an early seating (6 p.m.) in late winter and you get golden light through the windows of rooms like Elcielo on the river or The Surf Club by the ocean.
  • Find the contrast. A Bib Gourmand sandwich in a paper wrapper, held up against a Little Havana mural, says more about Miami than any white tablecloth.
  • At SOBEWFF, go wide. Tents on the sand, palm trees and smoke from the grills: a 24mm lens tells the scale of the festival better than a close-up of a canapé.
  • Respect the room. Many fine-dining restaurants discourage phones during service. One quiet frame per course, then put it away and eat. That is the point, after all.

Miami earned its stars by being itself: Latin, Japanese-obsessed, a little extravagant and hungry for more. The guide just put it in writing.

Questions people ask

How many Michelin-starred restaurants are in Miami in 2026?

Greater Miami and Miami Beach have 14 starred restaurants in the 2026 Michelin Guide Florida. L'Atelier de Joël Robuchon in the Design District holds two stars, and 13 restaurants hold one, including newcomer Mutra in North Miami, the first kosher restaurant in the world to earn a Michelin star.

When did the Michelin Guide come to Miami?

The Michelin Guide arrived in Florida on June 9, 2022, with a first edition covering Miami, Orlando and Tampa. That debut listed 118 restaurants statewide, with L'Atelier de Joël Robuchon as the only two-star and 10 one-star restaurants in Miami. In 2026 the guide expanded to cover the entire state.

What is a Bib Gourmand and which Miami restaurants have one?

A Bib Gourmand is Michelin's award for high-quality cooking at good value. Miami has 18 in 2026, including Tâm Tâm, Sanguich De Miami, Phuc Yea, El Turco and Chug's Diner, plus four new additions: Barra Callao, Cotoa, Double Luck and To Be Determined.

When is the South Beach Wine & Food Festival 2027?

The 26th South Beach Wine & Food Festival takes place February 25–28, 2027, in Miami Beach, according to the festival and Greater Miami Convention & Visitors Bureau listings. It is the city's biggest food weekend, with walk-around tastings on the sand, chef dinners and late-night parties.
