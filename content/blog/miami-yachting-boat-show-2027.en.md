Saturday, 11 a.m., off Haulover Inlet. Center consoles idle in a lazy circle, a 100-foot motor yacht slides past with its crew in matching polos, and somebody's speaker is losing a battle with somebody else's. The water is the color of a swimming pool filter commercial. Ten miles south, in Biscayne National Park, a manatee surfaces next to a kayak and nobody says a word.

Both scenes are Miami. **The city's yachting culture peaks every February at the [Discover Boating Miami International Boat Show](https://www.miamiboatshow.com/press/tickets-on-sale-for-the-2027-discover-boating-miami-international-boat-show/), which returns February 10–14, 2027, across four venues in Miami Beach.** But the real show runs all year, on a bay that is half playground, half protected wilderness.

## When and where is the 2027 Miami Boat Show?

The organizers confirmed the dates in an August 2026 announcement: **February 10–14, 2027**. Hours are Wednesday noon to 7 p.m., Thursday through Saturday 10 a.m. to 7 p.m., and Sunday 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. One ticket gets you into all four venues.

According to the show's exhibitor guide, those venues are:

| Venue | What you'll find |
|---|---|
| Miami Beach Convention Center | Indoor halls: boats, engines, electronics, gear |
| The Entry Point at Pride Park (new) | The front door and hub across from the convention center |
| Miami Beach Yacht Collection, Collins Avenue | In-water yachts along Indian Creek |
| Sailor's Cove at IGY Yacht Haven Grande, MacArthur Causeway | Sailboats and big yachts on Biscayne Bay |

The show is owned by the National Marine Manufacturers Association (NMMA) and the International Yacht Brokers Association (IYBA), and produced by Informa Markets. The 2026 edition was the 85th, which makes February 2027 the 86th. Spanish-language releases from the organizers listed adult tickets from $30 and youth tickets from $17, with children under 5 free; check the official site, since prices vary by day and package.

!! About 100,000 visitors: the 2025 show's attendance, with 41% traveling from outside Florida, according to industry reports on NMMA figures.

## Whatever happened to the Miami Yacht Show?

For decades, Miami had two February shows that felt like rival siblings. The boat show, run by NMMA, did not allow brokerage (pre-owned) yachts. So in **1989** the yacht brokers built their own: a brokerage show that first set up at Sunset Harbor and then moved to the docks along Collins Avenue. It went through several names, including Yachts Miami Beach and later the Miami Yacht Show, and grew into a superyacht magnet.

In **2022**, the two shows merged into one event under the Miami International Boat Show name ([background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_International_Boat_Show)). The result is what you see today: production boats and fishing machines in the convention center, and the floating real estate of the brokerage world parked on Indian Creek and the bay.

> In Miami, a boat is not a vehicle. It's a second address.

## How big is boating in Florida?

Big enough to rank first in the country, by a lot.

- **Registered vessels.** Florida had **1,027,742 registered vessels in 2025**, the most of any state, according to FWC figures reported by [Suncoast News](https://www.mysuncoast.com/2026/05/20/florida-records-694-boating-accidents-51-fatalities-2025/). Miami-Dade has long led Florida's counties: FWC counted 73,663 recreational vessels there in 2022 ([NBC Miami](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/local/florida-leads-the-nation-in-recreational-boats-see-which-county-has-the-most-registered-boats/3062078/)).
- **Economic weight.** NMMA describes Florida's recreational boating economy as roughly **$31 billion** a year ([NMMA, 2025](https://www.nmma.org/press/article/24979)), number one in the nation. Its most recent state study (2023) credits the sector with nearly 110,000 jobs and more than 7,100 businesses in Florida.
- **The show itself.** NMMA said the 2025 boat show generated nearly **$1 billion** in regional impact. An earlier independent study by Thomas J. Murray & Associates and the University of Florida put the annual figure at $854.2 million, supporting about 6,000 jobs ([Trade Only Today](https://tradeonlytoday.com/boat-shows/study-miami-international-boat-show-has-854-million-impact-on-florida/)).

!! 1,027,742 registered vessels: Florida's total in 2025, the most of any U.S. state (FWC).

## What makes Biscayne Bay special?

Here's the part visitors often miss: the turquoise water right in front of downtown is connected to a national park. [Biscayne National Park](https://www.nps.gov/bisc/index.htm) protects **172,971 acres**, and about **95% of it is water**. It started as a national monument in 1968 and became a national park in 1980.

The park covers four connected ecosystems: the mangrove shoreline, the shallow waters of the bay, the coral limestone keys at the top of the Florida Keys, and the northern stretch of the Florida Reef. Out on Boca Chita Key, a 65-foot ornamental lighthouse offers one of the best views of the Miami skyline, and it is reachable only by boat. Underwater, the Maritime Heritage Trail lets divers and snorkelers visit historic shipwrecks within the park.

!! 95% water: the share of Biscayne National Park's 172,971 acres that lies beneath the surface (NPS).

## Sandbars: Miami's floating living room

The sandbars are where Miami's boating culture turns social. **Haulover sandbar**, just inside Haulover Inlet, between Bal Harbour and Sunny Isles, is the weekend party: dozens of boats rafted together, music, floats, waist-deep water. **Nixon sandbar**, off Key Biscayne, is a bit calmer, with the skyline as backdrop.

A few rules of the road (literally):

1. **Watch the tide.** Sandbars change shape with the tides. Anchor at high tide without checking the chart and you may spend the afternoon aground.
2. **Anchor on sand, never on seagrass.** Anchors and propellers scar seagrass beds that take years to recover.
3. **Respect no-wake zones** around anchored boats and swimmers. Marine patrols from FWC and Miami-Dade are common on busy weekends.
4. **Designate a captain.** Boating under the influence is illegal in Florida, and 2025 saw 694 reportable boating accidents and 51 deaths statewide, per FWC.

## Is the bay in trouble?

Honestly, it has been. In August 2020, a major fish kill in northern Biscayne Bay was linked by scientists to dangerously low oxygen caused by nutrient pollution from sources like stormwater, fertilizer runoff and leaking septic systems. Seagrass, the bay's nursery and filter, has suffered large losses over recent decades.

The response: Miami-Dade's Biscayne Bay Task Force recommended stronger pollution rules, a management board and a dedicated Chief Bay Officer ([WLRN, 2020](https://www.wlrn.org/2020-08-18/biscayne-bay-needs-better-pollution-rules-an-oversight-board-and-a-chief-officer-task-force-says)), and the county created that post. Fish kills recurred in 2021 and 2022, a reminder that the work is far from done.

What you can do from the deck:

- Use sunscreen that is labeled reef-friendly, and don't toss anything overboard (including "biodegradable" stuff).
- Pump out at marinas, never into the bay.
- Slow down in manatee zones, which are posted and enforced.
- Join a cleanup with local groups such as Miami Waterkeeper.

## How do you experience the water without owning a yacht?

The dirty secret of yachting: most of the joy is available by the hour.

- **Captained charters.** Half-day charters to the sandbars or Stiltsville are the classic move. Split with friends and the math gets reasonable.
- **Boat clubs and peer-to-peer rentals.** Membership clubs and rental apps let you skipper a center console without the maintenance bill (you'll need a Florida boating education card if born on or after January 1, 1988).
- **The national park.** Guided boat and paddling trips in Biscayne National Park cost a fraction of a charter and include the best views.
- **Paddle it.** Kayaks and paddleboards from Key Biscayne or the Rickenbacker Causeway put you at water level with rays and manatees.
- **Go to the show.** Many yachts at the boat show can be toured. Even if you never sign a check, walking the docks at Yacht Haven Grande is a lesson in naval architecture.

## Through the lens

Miami's water is one of the most photogenic subjects in the country. Some field notes:

- **Golden hour, low angle.** Shoot yachts at sunrise from the MacArthur Causeway or at sunset from the Rickenbacker. Low sun brings out the color of the water and the lines of the hulls.
- **Use a polarizer.** It cuts glare and reveals the sandbars, seagrass and shades of turquoise beneath the surface.
- **Go up (legally).** Drone rules are strict near airports, marinas and the national park; drones are prohibited from launching inside national parks. Check FAA and NPS rules before you fly.
- **At the boat show, shoot details.** Teak decks, chrome cleats, stitched upholstery: the craft is in the close-ups. Arrive Wednesday afternoon or Thursday morning for fewer crowds.
- **Tell the human story.** A captain coiling a line or a kid spotting a manatee says more about Miami's water culture than any 200-foot hull.

Miami was built facing the water. Every February the boats come in; the rest of the year, the bay is waiting for you to go out.
