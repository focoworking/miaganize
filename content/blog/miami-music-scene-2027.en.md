Sunrise on a Sunday in downtown Miami. The bay turns from ink to pink, the first joggers hit Biscayne Boulevard, and on an open-air terrace a few blocks inland, a few hundred people who started dancing Saturday night are still going. Somewhere across town a reggaeton producer is opening a studio door, and a label executive is reading Monday's streaming numbers early. Miami music never really clocks out.

**The short answer:** Miami's music scene runs on three engines. Electronic music peaks with Miami Music Week and [Ultra Music Festival](https://ultramusicfestival.com/tickets/miami/), whose 27th edition is set for March 26–28, 2027 at Bayfront Park. The Latin music industry is headquartered here, from major label divisions to the Latin Recording Academy. And a dense map of venues, from 24-hour clubs to arenas, keeps the calendar full from November through March, the city's high season.

!! 27 editions: Ultra Music Festival reaches its 27th edition at Bayfront Park, March 26–28, 2027.

## When are Miami Music Week and Ultra 2027?

Ultra has confirmed March 26–28, 2027 at Bayfront Park on its [official site](https://ultramusicfestival.com/tickets/miami/). The Phase 1 lineup, released in mid-October 2026, features headliners including Calvin Harris, Martin Garrix and Armin van Buuren, plus Eric Prydz returning as his techno alias Cirez D, according to coverage by DJ Mag and Beatportal. More phases usually follow.

Around it, [Miami Music Week](https://www.miamiandbeaches.com/events/miami-music-week-guide) is listed for March 22–28, 2027. Think of it as a seven-day city takeover: hotel pool parties in Miami Beach, label showcases in Wynwood, marathon sets downtown.

The roots go back to the Winter Music Conference, founded in the mid-1980s as a gathering for DJs and the dance industry. Ultra itself began in 1999 as an offshoot of that conference week. The "Miami Music Week" name took hold around 2011–2012, and in 2018 Ultra's organizers acquired the WMC.

> In Miami, a festival is never one weekend. It's the week that bends the whole city around it.

## Why is Miami the capital of the Latin music industry?

Because the offices are here. Sony's Latin division traces its Miami story to 1980, when CBS set up CBS Discos in the city; it later became Sony Discos and then Sony Music Latin. [Florida Trend](https://www.floridatrend.com/article/13870/the-sound-of-money/) documented how Warner's Latin arm moved its headquarters from Los Angeles to Miami in the early 1990s, and how industry insiders began calling the city "the Nashville of Latin music." Warner Music Latina remains based in Miami today.

The institutions followed. The Latin Recording Academy, founded in 1997 to run the Latin Grammys, is headquartered in Miami. The 2026 ceremony takes place November 12 at the MGM Grand Garden Arena in Las Vegas, [per Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/2026/10/20/rosalia-daddy-yankee-anitta-to-perform-at-2026-latin-grammys/), with Daddy Yankee honored as Person of the Year at a gala the night before; the 2024 edition returned the show to Miami. Meanwhile, [Billboard Latin Music Week](https://wsvn.com/entertainment/deco-drive/billboard-latin-music-week-set-to-return-to-miami-beach-in-october-with-weeklong-events/), in its 37th year after starting as a business conference in 1990, ran October 19–22, 2026 at the Faena Forum in Miami Beach.

!! 1980: the year CBS opened its Latin music division in Miami, the seed of today's Sony Music Latin.

### The sound of the streets

The industry follows the street, not the other way around. Miami is a crossroads of Cuban son and salsa, Puerto Rican and Dominican reggaeton and dembow, Haitian kompa from Little Haiti, Colombian and Venezuelan pop, and Brazilian funk. Producers and artists from across the Caribbean and Latin America base themselves here for the flights, the studios and the proximity to the labels, which is why a Monday writing session in Miami can turn into a global single by Friday.

[Club Space](https://news.pollstar.com/2025/03/28/25-years-of-club-space-iconic-miami-venue-reflects-on-a-quarter-century-of-24-hour-parties) deserves its own paragraph. It opened in March 2000, made possible by a city vote that created a small 24-hour entertainment district. Pollstar, marking its 25th anniversary in 2025, described it as running 24 hours on weekends, with marathon parties stretching to 36 hours around Miami Music Week and Art Basel. The Terrace at sunrise is one of the city's genuine rites of passage. Schedules shift season to season, so check the club's official channels before you go.

[Hard Rock Live](https://www.seminolehardrockhollywood.com/newsroom/2019/10/seminole-hard-rock-hotel--casino-hollywood-to-debut-125-million-hard-rock-live), a 7,000-capacity venue at the guitar-shaped Seminole Hard Rock in Hollywood, opened in 2019 as a $125 million project. The Fillmore Miami Beach, in the former Jackie Gleason Theater, has been run by Live Nation since 2007 and holds about 2,700 for concerts.

!! 24 hours: how long Club Space runs on weekends, which Pollstar called unique in the U.S. (2025).

## What happened to Rolling Loud?

Rolling Loud is the ultimate Miami origin story. It began in February 2015 as a one-day show at Soho Studios in Wynwood, moved to Mana Wynwood in 2016, Bayfront Park in 2017 and Hard Rock Stadium in 2018, becoming one of the world's biggest hip-hop festivals. In 2026, organizers took their only U.S. edition to Camping World Stadium in Orlando, May 8–10, [Miami New Times reported](https://www.miaminewtimes.com/music/rolling-loud-festival-ditches-miami-for-orlando-40515133/), citing accessibility and growth. No return to Miami has been announced. For a city that exports festivals as easily as it creates them, it reads less like a goodbye than a reminder: nothing here stays still.

## Through the lens

**For photographers:** Bayfront Park's stages look most dramatic at blue hour, when the LED walls balance with the downtown skyline; shoot from the edge of the crowd with a short telephoto to compress the stage against the towers. At Club Space, the story is the Terrace at dawn: faces lit by the first sun, not the strobes. Always ask before photographing people up close, and check each venue's camera policy, since many limit professional gear without a press pass.

**For listeners:** do Miami Music Week like a local. Pick one neighborhood per day, go to one pool party and one club night, not five. Leave a weekday for a Latin show at the Fillmore or Kaseya Center. And at least once, stay until sunrise. The city sounds different at 6 a.m.
