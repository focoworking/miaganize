From the Rickenbacker Causeway at blue hour, Miami's skyline looks like a graphic equalizer: bars of light rising and falling over the bay. Look twice, though, and something has shifted. A crane that no longer has the Downtown sky to itself. A Brickell tower that suddenly outranks everything around it. Miami is changing altitude, and you can see it with the naked eye.

The short answer: 2026 is Miami's biggest vertical leap yet. [Cipriani Residences](https://www.thorntontomasetti.com/news/cipriani-residences-miami-tops-out-redefining-brickell-skyline) hit 950 feet in July to become the tallest tower in Florida; the Waldorf Astoria Miami will be the city's first supertall (taller than 300 meters); and down at street level, the city keeps defending its Art Deco and MiMo heritage while starchitects sign museums, parking garages and even a soccer stadium.

## What is the tallest building in Miami right now?

For nearly a decade the crown belonged to Panorama Tower in Brickell, at 868 feet. Before that it was the Four Seasons (789 feet), from 2003 to 2017. In April 2026, Cipriani Residences passed the mark at 872 feet, per [Florida YIMBY](https://floridayimby.com/2026/04/cipriani-residences-miami-reaches-872-feet-becomes-miamis-tallest-residential-tower.html), and in July it reached its final height of 950 feet. Developer Mast Capital is targeting delivery in summer 2027.

!! 950 feet: the final height of Cipriani Residences, now Florida's tallest tower (Thornton Tomasetti, July 2026).

The reign will be short. A few blocks north on Biscayne Boulevard, the Waldorf Astoria Hotel & Residences is climbing to 100 stories and 1,049 feet, designed by Sieger Suarez Architects with Uruguayan architect Carlos Ott. Its stack of offset cubes, as if someone nudged each one with a fingertip, is the defining image of the new Miami. According to [Dezeen](https://www.dezeen.com/2025/12/10/miami-supertall-skyscraper-waldorf-astoria-hotel-residences-construction/), it is now aiming for 2028, two years past its original target. Newsweek reported it passed the 75th floor in May 2026, and Florida YIMBY expects it to top out between late 2026 and early 2027.

!! 1,049 feet: the height of the Waldorf Astoria Miami, the city's first supertall (Dezeen, 2025).

> Miami has stopped growing toward the sea. It is growing up, and in a hurry.

## The towers redrawing the horizon

| Waldorf Astoria Hotel & Residences | Downtown | 1,049 ft / 100 floors | Under construction; delivery targeted 2028 |
| Cipriani Residences Miami | Brickell | 950 ft / 80 floors | Topped out (July 2026); delivery summer 2027 |

| Aston Martin Residences | Downtown, mouth of the Miami River | ~816 ft / 66 floors | Completed 2024 |
| One Thousand Museum (Zaha Hadid) | Downtown | 709 ft / 62 floors | Completed |

**Okan Tower**, from Turkey's Okan Group, is Downtown's other big bet: 72 stories on North Miami Avenue. By April 2026 it had passed the 50th floor, roughly 75% of its structure, according to [Florida YIMBY](https://floridayimby.com/2026/04/construction-reaches-50th-floor-on-okan-tower-at-555-north-miami-avenue-in-downtown-miami.html).

**Aston Martin Residences** was the first of this new generation to cross the finish line: 66 stories of curved glass at the mouth of the Miami River, completed in 2024 according to [Florida YIMBY](https://floridayimby.com/2024/05/aston-martin-residences-completes-construction-at-300-biscayne-boulevard-way-in-downtown-miami.html). It is already a must-shoot postcard from Brickell Key.

And **One Brickell City Centre**, the supertall office tower Swire Properties and Related unveiled with fanfare in 2022? According to [CoStar](https://www.costar.com/article/1665509297/miami-supertall-office-tower-said-to-be-canceled-after-preleasing-fails-to-materialize), it was reported canceled in early 2025 after preleasing failed to materialize. A useful reminder: in Miami, a rendering is not a tower.

## Why is Art Deco still the soul of Miami Beach?

Long before the supertalls, Miami had an architecture all its own, pastel and streamlined. On May 14, 1979, the Miami Beach Art Deco District was listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the nation's first urban 20th-century historic district, with some 800 contributing buildings, according to the [Miami Design Preservation League](https://mdpl.org/archives/2021/05/national-register-of-historic-places) (MDPL). The force behind it was Barbara Baer Capitman, MDPL's founder, who fought building by building when demolition looked inevitable.

!! 800 buildings: listed on the National Register in 1979 as part of Miami Beach's Art Deco District (MDPL).

The style's big party is **Art Deco Weekend**, which MDPL has staged since 1977. The next edition, the 50th, runs **January 15–17, 2027** (MLK weekend) along Ocean Drive and in Lummus Park, from 5th Street to 14th Place, according to [Greater Miami & Miami Beach](https://www.miamiandbeaches.com/event/art-deco-weekend/95?category=2). If you only ever go once, make it this one: half a century of classic cars, talks, tours and people dressed like it's 1935.

## MiMo: modernism with a beach umbrella

If Deco is 1930s Miami, **MiMo** (Miami Modern) is the postwar version: floating canopies, boomerang curves, neon signs promising an endless vacation. Morris Lapidus wrote its manifesto with the Fontainebleau (1954) and the Eden Roc (1955), both on Collins Avenue, inside the Morris Lapidus / Mid-20th Century Historic District that Miami Beach designated in 2009.

On the mainland, the City of Miami's [MiMo Historic District](https://www.miami.gov/My-Government/Departments/Planning/Historic-Preservation-Main-Page/MiMo-Historic-District) runs along Biscayne Boulevard from NE 50th to 77th streets, designated in June 2006. The stars here are roadside motels like the Vagabond (1953), whose sign is still one of the best night shots in the city.

## Starchitects at street level

### Pérez Art Museum Miami (PAMM)
Herzog & de Meuron opened PAMM in December 2013, during Art Basel week, in Museum Park. It's a museum built for the climate: a broad platform under a shading roof, slender columns and hanging gardens by French botanist Patrick Blanc, with about 80 plant species. The same firm had already delivered **1111 Lincoln Road** in 2010, the Miami Beach parking garage that looks like a concrete house of cards.

### Miami Design District
The neighborhood Craig Robins turned into a luxury showcase is also an open-air architecture museum. The **Fly's Eye Dome**, a reproduction of the 24-foot geodesic dome Buckminster Fuller patented in 1965, has anchored Palm Court since 2014. Nearby: **Sou Fujimoto**'s blue-glass façade and **Aranda\Lasch**'s embroidered-concrete pavilion. Then there's the **Museum Garage** (2018): Terence Riley borrowed the surrealist "exquisite corpse" game and invited WORKac, J. Mayer H., Clavel Arquitectos (with 36 car shells painted silver and gold) and Nicolas Buffe to each design a face, per [ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/897283/miami-museum-garage-workac-plus-nicolas-buffe-plus-clavel-arquitectos-plus-k-r-and-j-mayer-h).

### Faena District
In Mid-Beach, Alan Faena gathered Foster + Partners (Faena House, 18 oceanfront stories) and Rem Koolhaas's OMA (Faena Forum, Faena Bazaar and a parking structure) around the old Saxony Hotel, restored as the Faena Hotel and opened in November 2015. It's the most theatrical stretch of Miami Beach.

## Nu Stadium: architecture gets in the game

The project long known as Miami Freedom Park now has its stadium and its sponsor name: **Nu Stadium**, next to Miami International Airport. It opened April 4, 2026, with a 2-2 draw against Austin FC, with goals from Messi and Luis Suárez, per the [club](https://intermiamicf.com/news/match-recap-inter-miami-cf-plays-historic-opener-at-nu-stadium). Official capacity is about 25,000 seats, and the stadium is just one piece of a 131-acre development that includes a 58-acre public park, offices, retail and a hotel.

It's a shift in scale for the west side of the city: for once, a high-profile piece of architecture lands far from the bay and the beach.

## Through the lens

- **The full skyline:** the Rickenbacker Causeway and Virginia Key give you the head-on view of Brickell and Downtown. Go at blue hour, about 20 minutes after sunset, when the towers light up and the sky still holds color. Tripod and a long exposure to smooth the water.
- **The Waldorf under construction:** shoot it now. A half-built supertall, cranes and open cubes and all, is an image that won't come back. From Maurice A. Ferré Park (next to PAMM) you get the tower in context with the bay.
- **Art Deco:** Ocean Drive at sunrise, before the tables and cars, for clean façades; at night for the neon. At Art Deco Weekend, bring a 35mm lens and patience with the crowds.
- **MiMo:** the Vagabond sign and the Biscayne motels call for night and a fast lens.
- **Design District:** early morning, before shops open, the Fly's Eye Dome and Museum Garage are people-free. Remember it's private property: ask first for commercial shoots.
- **Drones:** almost all of this skyline sits inside controlled airspace around MIA. Without FAA authorization (and, in Miami Beach, a city permit), leave the drone at home.

Miami is not a city that holds still for the picture. Which is exactly why you should take it now.
